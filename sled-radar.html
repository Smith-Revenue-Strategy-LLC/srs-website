Two sides of the same bid table

One finds the work. The other decides if it is worth chasing.

Two systems get built here. They look like opposites and they are. BidStrike starts after the invitation lands and answers which ones deserve your estimator's hours. SLED Radar starts before an invitation exists at all, and finds the public work while it is still a line in a set of meeting minutes.

Same conviction underneath both. The bottleneck was never the data. It was the decision, and who had time to make it.

The split

BidStrike

Downstream. For specialty contractors.

The work comes to you. Invitations arrive faster than anyone can read them, and the pile does not sort itself. Most of it is noise. Some of it is the job that pays for the year.

  • Ranks live invitations against your real capacity
  • Weighs the relationship capital you already hold with that general contractor
  • Runs the go/no-go triage your team does by hand today
  • Hands the estimator a short list instead of an inbox

The question it answers: what deserves the hours?

SLED Radar

Upstream. For general contractors chasing public work.

The work does not come to you. By the time a state, city, or school district publishes a solicitation, the shortlist is often already forming. The signal was public months earlier. It was just sitting in records nobody had time to read.

  • Watches land acquisition and property transfers
  • Reads school board and city council minutes
  • Tracks master plans, enrollment studies, and demographic shifts
  • Follows bond and millage activity, because the money moves first

The question it answers: what is coming before it is announced?

Why they belong together

Opposite ends, one problem.

A specialty contractor drowns in invitations and cannot tell which ones are real. A general contractor pursuing public work has the opposite problem. Nothing arrives. You go find it, or you read about the award later.

Both are the same failure wearing different clothes. The information was available. Nobody had the hours to turn it into a decision. That is the gap both systems close, from opposite directions.

The part that matters

This does not replace your capture person.

Right now, whether your firm sees an opportunity early depends on whether one person happened to have a slow week and read the minutes. That is not a strategy. That is luck with a job title.

SLED Radar does not do their job. It does the reading, the sorting, and the scoring, so the person doing capture spends their hours where a machine is useless: in the room, with the owner, with the architect, with the people who decide. The relationship is still the whole game. The system just stops the pipeline from depending on who had time.

Where it stands

Phase 1 is in build.

SLED Radar is being built now against one state's public records, with Phase 1 go-live targeted for September 2026. It is not a concept deck. It is a system with a scope, a schedule, and a first set of records already being read.

The approach travels. Every state runs its own public-notice, bond, and open-meeting rules, and those rules are what the system reads. If you are a general contractor pursuing state, local, and education work outside the first build state, the next builds are open. Bring the market you chase and we will map what is actually public in it before anyone talks scope.
