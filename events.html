AI Collab - Waco

Real AI skills, learned in the room.

AI Collab - Waco is a hands-on AI workshop series for Waco owners, operators, and builders. Small groups, real problems, and a plan you can use Monday morning. I also run private sessions for a single team, built around your own workflows.

What I have done

I ran the first one in June, and the working time was the point.

The first AI Collab workshop ran June 25 and 26 at 25N Coworking in downtown Waco. Two afternoons, hands-on, with tables grouped by skill level so builders worked with builders and operators worked with operators. Half of it was teaching. The other half was guided working time on the attendee's own business, which is where the useful part happened.

  • Format Eight hours across two afternoons, with guided working time on real problems.
  • Group Small tables by experience level, with guide support kept close.

What we actually ran, block by block

Day one, Thursday June 25

  1. 1:00 Framing the why. Who built this, and the crawl-walk-run shape of the two days.
  2. 1:20 Building your table. A structured mixer so the peer work later actually works.
  3. 1:45 Foundations. What AI is and is not, in plain language. Model, prompt, token, context window.
  4. 3:00 Lab one, your first useful prompt. Everyone picks a tedious weekly task and rebuilds it.
  5. 4:00 Lab two, the safety scan. A decision framework for company, client, and proprietary data.
  6. 4:50 Wrap and homework.

Day two, Friday June 26

  1. 1:00 Kickoff and homework share-outs. One win, one thing that did not work.
  2. 1:10 Pick your path, round one. Five tracks, self-selected by role and by the problem you brought.
  3. 2:45 Governance, budgets, observability. What tokens actually cost, in plain English.
  4. 3:25 Pick your path, round two. Go deeper or pivot, applied to your own situation.
  5. 4:40 Your AI first 30. Three changes and the first Monday action, written down.

The spine under all of it was one ratio on the whiteboard: 60 / 30 / 10. Sixty percent of the result comes from the files you feed the thing, thirty percent from the rules you give it, ten percent from the model's own judgment. Most people spend all their energy on the ten percent.

What people left with

A seat came with work product, not a workbook.

I am not going to publish anyone's files. They belong to the people who built them. Here is what the shape of a leave-behind is, so you know what a seat buys.

Your own AI workspace

Not notes. A folder that carries your business: who you are, how you work, the projects you are running, and what the assistant already learned about you. Your AI reads it before it answers anything.

Your AI opportunity map

A one-pager built in the room off your own intake. Three rungs, from the chat box to a system that keeps watch, and what each rung unlocks in your job specifically. Not a generic ladder.

Real work, finished in the room

Not exercises. Sourced market research, pricing guardrails, and ranked lead lists with names, titles, and where to go meet them. Sitting in the attendee's own folder when they walked out the door.
