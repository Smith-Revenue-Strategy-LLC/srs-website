Who you would be working with

Rodney Smith

Revenue and workflow consultant in Waco. I help owners fix the systems that are costing them time.

I didn't set out to be a workflow consultant.

I've worked inside sales organizations that ran like a well-oiled machine. Clear priorities, clean systems, aligned teams. Work moved forward, people had margin, and there was energy left at the end of the day.

I've also worked inside the opposite. Layered approvals, broken handoffs, constant friction. Everything felt harder than it should be. And what stood out most wasn't the business impact. It was what that strain did to people. You could feel it follow them home.

That contrast stuck with me. How a business runs on the inside doesn't just affect performance. It shapes the lives of the people inside it. Their stress, their time, their presence with family. It's all connected.

That's why I do this work. I help organizations remove friction, simplify workflows, and build systems that actually support the people using them. Not just so the business can grow, but so the people in it can breathe a little more when they walk through their front door at night.

Because better systems don't just build better companies. They build better lives.

I should say where that comes from. I'm a Christian, and Scripture is direct about it. God's heart is for the freedom of the captive. I don't think that's only about the big things. A person spending four hours on work a machine should have handled has none of it, and their family feels that before they do.

Freedom is the point. The systems are just how I get there. My faith and my family come first, and everything else is downstream of that.

That conviction is also why I work with small and midsize companies. I left corporate in December. The owners I want to work with have worn twelve hats and bootstrapped for a living, and that is the room I want to be in.

In one of those enterprise years I did two jobs because the system couldn't do one. We had money. Jessica did not have a husband. My kids had a father, but a ghost of one.

So now I work with owners. I fix the tools so the business grows and you get your evenings back.

This size is where a change can actually ship. The person who decides is in the room, and it shows up in the pipeline in weeks instead of quarters. What is hard here is not the options. There are endless options. It is sifting them with a business already running. That sifting is my part of the work, so your team gets back to the human side of theirs and nobody has to become an AI expert on their own time to stay competitive.

I'll tell you if the problem isn't ready to solve.

Here's what that looks like in practice.

  • 17 years in go-to-market roles, all of them on outcomes-based comp. 8x President's Club across selling, management, and sales training.
  • Rebuilt hiring and onboarding SOPs across a global team and cut new-hire ramp time by about half.
  • Served as the subject-matter expert on four enterprise platform rollouts, driving adoption across 112 users and pulling them into one system of record.
  • Led a global team to 160% of target and nearly $29M in new pipeline in a single year.

Right now I'm working with a mechanical contractor who gets 40 bid invites a day and loses about 10 hours a week just updating dates by hand. That's the kind of friction I build systems to remove.

How I use AI in my own work.

I use AI tools for the organizing, drafting, and research work. That's what they're good at. But everything you get from me, I've read and I stand behind. The composition gets automated. The accountability doesn't.

I am not flying solo

This practice grows under outside counsel, not around it. I stay in submission to advisors who have run bigger rooms than I have, and I take their input before I take my own word for it.

I also know when the right move is to hand something to a partner who does it better than I would. Cybersecurity. Local technology and hardware support. Website, branding, and wordsmithing. Executive coaching. Naming that line is part of the job.
